Setting up your withdrawal

Before you start

  • We've sent you a Retirement Pack with the heading 'Helping you plan for your retirement', which outlines options you need to consider when taking money out of your pension. Please read this and bear in mind that your circumstances may have changed over time. If your letter is more than 12 months old, you'll need to request an up-to-date version. Please call us on 0800 3 68 68 68 to request your new letter.

  • Check that your personal details are up to date on your account to avoid any delay in processing your withdrawal request. To help protect you and Fidelity against financial crime, we need to verify your identity and run an electronic check on the bank account you'll be receiving the funds into. During these checks, we use a credit reference agency to validate your details. It leaves a soft footprint on your credit file, which means it's visible to you and us but no other parties. This will not have any impact on your credit rating.

What to expect once you begin

  • You'll need to read some important information and answer some questions. These are in place to help you consider and understand the impact that a withdrawal will make on your future retirement income.

  • Tell us how much you want to take out. We'll then show you the tax you'll need to pay if any is due.

  • Decide what you want to do with any money left after your withdrawal. You can choose another investment option or leave it where it is in your account.

  • Download and review your 'Illustration' - this a personalised summary of the choices you've made regarding your withdrawal request.

Do you know..

In UK pension planning, terms like UFPLS, Crystallised, and Uncrystallised dictate retirement income strategies. UFPLS allows withdrawals directly from uncrystallised funds (not yet committed to drawdown/annuities), offering 25% tax-free and 75% taxable. Once funds are crystallised (e.g., allocated to drawdown or annuities), they lose flexibility and cannot be used for UFPLS.

Strategic use is vital: excessive UFPLS withdrawals may increase tax liability, while crystallising too early limits future options. Balancing uncrystallised and crystallised funds optimizes tax efficiency and financial flexibility.
